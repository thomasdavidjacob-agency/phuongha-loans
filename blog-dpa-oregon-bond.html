Down Payment Assistance

Oregon's Bond Residential Loan Program: a below-market rate and real help at closing.

By Phuong Ha · NMLS #1839449 · June 2026

If you're buying your first home in Oregon — or you haven't owned one in the last three years — the Oregon Bond Residential Loan Program is one of the most powerful tools available to you. Most people have never heard of it. That's the whole reason I keep writing these posts.

What the program actually does

Oregon Housing and Community Services (OHCS) issues bonds and uses the proceeds to offer mortgage rates that are typically below the going market rate. That's the first benefit: a lower interest rate just for qualifying. You pair that with either a conventional, FHA, or VA loan.

The second piece is optional but significant: down payment assistance. OHCS offers a grant — money that doesn't have to be repaid — toward your down payment and closing costs. The exact percentage is set by the state and can change, but it's real money that reduces what you need to bring to the table on closing day.

Who it's for

The program is aimed at moderate-income buyers purchasing a primary residence in Oregon. Key checkboxes:

  • First-time buyer status — not having owned a home in the past three years. More people meet this than realize.
  • Income limits — these vary by county and household size. In the Portland metro they're higher than many assume.
  • Purchase price limits — there are caps, but they're set to reflect realistic Oregon market prices.
  • Credit profile — a minimum score is required, though it's not as high as some people fear.

Rate + assistance: a combination worth running the numbers on

Here's what makes this program stand out: you're not just getting help with the down payment. You're also locking in a rate that may be lower than anything a conventional lender quotes you that week. Over 30 years, that rate difference compounds into a meaningful amount of money — sometimes tens of thousands of dollars.

A lower rate and a down payment grant together can change what "affordable" means for you.
