Down Payment Assistance

Oregon Bond is becoming Flex Lending: what FirstHome and NextStep mean for you.

By Phuong Ha · NMLS #1839449 · Updated August 2026

If someone told you to ask about the Oregon Bond Residential Loan Program, that advice was right — but the name has changed. Oregon Housing and Community Services (OHCS) is phasing that program out and replacing it with Flex Lending, which comes in two flavors: FirstHome and NextStep.

The good news is that the thing that made Oregon Bond worth chasing is still there. Here's what actually changed, and one detail that a lot of older articles online still get wrong.

The part that didn't change

OHCS issues bonds and uses the proceeds to offer mortgage rates that are typically below the going market rate. That's still the core benefit: a lower interest rate just for qualifying, on a primary residence in Oregon. Over the life of a loan, that difference compounds into real money.

Assistance toward your down payment and closing costs is still part of the package too. What changed is how it's structured, and how you qualify.

FirstHome vs. NextStep

FirstHome is the closer descendant of Oregon Bond. It's built for first-time buyers, and it comes with down payment assistance. The program sets the assistance amount and it can change, so ask me for the current figure. "First-time" has the usual meaning — not having owned a home in the past three years — and there are exceptions: veterans and previous homeowners buying in designated Targeted Areas can also qualify.

NextStep is the more flexible one, and it's the option most people don't know exists. It has no first-time homebuyer requirement at all. Qualification is more forgiving, and there's a household income cap.

If you've owned a home before and assumed these programs were closed to you, NextStep is the reason to ask anyway.

The detail most articles get wrong

This is the important one. A lot of writing about Oregon's programs — including older versions of this very page — describes the assistance as a grant, meaning money you never pay back.

That is not how Flex Lending assistance is structured. OHCS describes the down payment assistance as a loan that is either repayable or forgivable, depending on the product and terms. Forgivable is genuinely good — it can go away over time — but forgivable is not the same as free, and you should know which one you're signing before you sign it.

Forgivable is not the same as a grant. Ask which one you're getting, and what makes it forgivable.

What the assistance can do is substantial: OHCS allows those funds to cover up to 100% of your cash requirement to close — down payment, closing costs, prepaid items, upfront mortgage insurance, and related fees. For a lot of buyers that's the entire barrier, gone.

Who tends to qualify

  • Income limits — these vary by product, county, and household size. NextStep has its own cap; FirstHome's vary. In the Portland metro they're higher than many assume.
  • Purchase price limits — there are caps, set to reflect realistic Oregon market prices.
  • Primary residence — somewhere you'll actually live.
  • Credit profile — a minimum applies, though it's usually not as high as people fear.

Why this transition is worth a conversation

Program transitions are exactly when buyers get bad information. Half the articles online still describe Oregon Bond as though nothing changed, and the assistance percentages, income caps, and forgiveness terms all move over time. What's written above is accurate as I write it, and it's worth confirming against current guidelines before you make decisions on it.
