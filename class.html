WHAT THE SHORT LIST LEAVES OUT

Four things most Oregon renters have never been told.

The decision usually gets made alone, against a short menu, and never revisited. Nobody ran the numbers for that county, that household, that situation. Here is some of what the short menu leaves out.

01

You may not need to be a first-time buyer.

Some programs have no first-time buyer requirement at all. Washington's Home Advantage is one. And for many programs that do have one, "first-time" only means you haven't owned a home in the last three years.

More: Washington's WSHFC programs
02

Help can go beyond the down payment.

Many assistance programs can be applied to more than the down payment, including closing costs and prepaid items. Closing costs surprise more buyers than the down payment does.

More: Assistance and closing costs
03

The county line can change your options.

Local programs exist on top of the state's, and they differ between Multnomah, Washington, Clackamas and Clark counties. Which side of a line you buy on is worth knowing before you narrow your search.

More: Portland–Vancouver county breakdown
04

You find these through a participating lender.

Assistance programs are delivered through participating lenders. I offer MSF National DPA through Mortgage Solutions Financial, in Oregon and Washington. Many programs also require a short homebuyer education course. Knowing the order of steps is most of what keeps a purchase on schedule.

More: MSF National DPA

And the honest part

Assistance is not free money, and it isn't right for everybody. A lot of assistance is a second loan on the home, not a grant. It may be forgivable or repaid, and it can come due if you sell, refinance or stop living there. Programs have eligibility rules, a credit minimum, and terms that change. The point of the class is to see the whole list and the real conditions, so you can decide with the full picture.
